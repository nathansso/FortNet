# Forecast Fortnite Decline Without Assuming Biology

Neurobiology predicts only a small, gradual loss of cognitive-motor speed that starts in the mid-20s. It cannot explain why Fortnite stars fade at 16 to 18. The best-measured analogue of "latent loop processing" is perception-action cycle latency: the time from taking in a new view to acting on it. In 3,305 StarCraft II players it began slowing at **age 24 (95% CI 20–29), by roughly 10 ms per year**, and expertise did not protect against it ([Thompson et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094215)). Fortnite's observed "peak" is far earlier. The modal age of prize earners is 16, and battle royale earnings peak at about 19 ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/fortnite/fortnites-peak-competitive-age-is-16/); [Kang 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/simgam/v57y2026i1p93-114.html)). The best-documented exits, Mongraal at about 16–17 and Benjyfishy at about 18, came with burnout, streaming income and switches to other titles, years before any plausible neural onset. Four non-biological forces explain the early peak better than aging does. The game launched in 2017, so every elite player started young. The format changes almost every season. Players drop out selectively. And one-off prize pools, such as the 2019 World Cup, distort earnings curves. No one has published a Fortnite placement or career forecasting model. That leaves a real gap for a student project, and the tools to fill it are mature: multi-player rating systems (Plackett-Luce, Elo-MMR, TrueSkill 2), aging-curve methods from baseball that correct for dropout, and survival models. The right project treats biology as one testable hypothesis inside a forecasting model, not as its premise. The model estimates age effects separately from tenure, cohort, format era and activity, and it is scored on how well it forecasts placements at Majors and LANs.

## "Latent loop processing" maps onto a measurable perception-action cycle

"Latent loop processing" is not an established term in neuroscience or esports science. No source in the literature search used it. The most defensible way to operationalize it is as a closed-loop **perception → decision → action cycle time**. Thompson, Blair and Henrey measured exactly this in StarCraft II as "looking-doing latency", the delay from a new screen fixation to the first action. Players went through about 300 of these cycles per game ([Thompson et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094215)). A 39-year-old was about **150 ms slower per cycle** than a 24-year-old at the same league level. That adds up to about 30 seconds over a 15-minute game, and it equals about 15% of the speed advantage pros hold over Bronze players ([Thompson et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094215)). Older players compensated: they used more hotkeys, attacked more often via the minimap, and chose less complex units. A dual-task measure (worker production) showed no age effect at all (p = .97).

The most useful decomposition for a data scientist comes from a diffusion-model analysis of about **1.2 million people's** speeded decisions. Reaction-time slowing starts around 20, but the individual components move in different directions. **Non-decision time** (perceptual encoding plus motor execution) rises linearly from about 16. **Decision caution** rises from about 18. The **drift rate**, the core "mental speed" of accumulating evidence, keeps improving until about 30 and holds steady until 60 ([von Krause et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01282-7); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/2022/04/mental-speed-doesnt-begin-to-decline-until-as-late-as-age-60-study-indicates-62973)). So a "loop speed" construct needs at least two parts. One is a motor/encoding latency term that drifts upward from the early 20s. The other is a caution term that also rises with age but is partly a strategic choice, not lost capacity.

Other capacities peak on different schedules. **Processing speed (digit-symbol coding) peaks around 18–19**, working memory around 25–30, and vocabulary at 50 or later ([Hartshorne & Germine 2015](https://local.psy.miami.edu/faculty/dmessinger/c_c/rsrcs/rdgs/cognitive/psychsci2015.pdf); [BPS digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/different-mental-abilities-peak-different-times-life-18-70)). Saccade latency is fastest, and least variable, in the 20–30 bracket ([Munoz et al. 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9746145/)). Simple reaction time, the "click when it turns green" test, **barely changes until about 50**, while choice RT slows across adulthood ([Der & Deary 2006](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/age-and-sex-differences-in-reaction-time-in-adulthood-results-fro/)). Simple RT is therefore a poor proxy. Complex, choice-laden cycles are the right target. Lab reaching data from 2,390 people gives lifespan slopes of **1.3 ms/year in reaction time, 4.3 ms/year in movement time and 0.04°/year in precision**, and age remained a predictor after controlling for video-game experience ([Communications Psychology 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00383-7)).

The neural substrates are a mixed and circumstantial story. Striatal D2/D3 dopamine receptor availability is **still declining across ages 18–30**, which makes it the most direct evidence that a basal-ganglia-loop parameter changes during the esports window ([Larsen et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-14693-3)). A meta-analysis puts dopamine receptor and transporter losses at **3.7–14% per decade** ([Karrer et al. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0197458017301616)), so the change over five years is small. White matter points the other way. Fractional anisotropy keeps rising until **20–42 depending on tract, and whole-brain FA peaks around 32** ([Lebel et al. 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053811911013760); [Kochunov et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20122755/)). The popular claim that myelin declines in your 20s is therefore wrong, because conduction-related microstructure is still maturing. No study found here links an individual's neural change between 18 and 30 to a change in gaming or visuomotor performance. Cortico-basal-ganglia and cerebellar internal-model loops are real circuits, but connecting them to Fortnite decline is currently speculation.

## Biology predicts a mid-20s drift, but Fortnite stars fade at 17

The honest verdict is that **the evidence does not support a biological explanation as the primary driver** of Fortnite's early peak. The timing does not fit. The SC2 onset estimate is 24 with a lower bound of 20, and an early deficit of tens of milliseconds is swamped by differences between players. Fortnite's decline stories cluster years earlier. Bugha won the 2019 World Cup at 16, won further FNCS titles at 18–19, and then slid to placements of mostly 5th–23rd in 2023 and 7th–14th in 2024. He is still a top-10 Major finisher at 22–23 ([Wikipedia: Bugha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugha_(gamer))), which looks like a plateau rather than a collapse. Mongraal "effectively retired" at about 16–17 citing burnout, then returned in 2023 when the Chapter 1 map came back ([Wikipedia: Mongraal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongraal)). Benjyfishy left for Valorant in 2022 after years of 8–10-hour days, citing burnout and lost enjoyment ([Esports News UK](https://esports-news.co.uk/2022/06/24/benjyfishy-retires-fortnite-valorant/)). None of these trajectories fits a neural decline that begins in the mid-20s.

Five non-biological mechanisms fit the pattern better, and each would produce an age-shaped curve in observational data.

The first is **cohort truncation**. Fortnite has existed only since 2017 and the entry age is 13 ([Wikipedia: FNCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Championship_Series)), so every elite player started as a teenager and nobody has had time to peak later. Chess shows the same artefact: grandmasters born in 2000 or later "peak" at **19.8–21.3**, against a population mean of 30.65, purely because of right-censoring ([Erilli & Dalar 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485108/)).

The second is **format churn**. Team size changed at least seven times between 2019 and 2026 (trios, squads, duos, solos, trios, duos, trios, duos). Movement was overhauled in Chapters 4 and 5, and 2026 brought a new "net surge" scoring rule ([Wikipedia: FNCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Championship_Series); [Fortnite Tracker](https://fortnitetracker.com/article/2389/fortnite-2026-competitive-news); [Hotspawn](https://www.hotspawn.com/fortnite/news/fncs-global-championship-2026)). Each reset favors newcomers who have no old habits to unlearn.

The third is **selective, voluntary exit**. Streaming audiences give veterans income that does not depend on results (Mongraal has 7M Twitch followers, Bugha 5.5M), so "decline" often means "stopped grinding".

The fourth is **event-driven earnings**. Bugha earned $3.08M in 2019 against about $96k in 2024, which says more about the 2019 World Cup than about aging ([Wikipedia: Bugha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugha_(gamer))).

The fifth is **physical wear**. Pain prevalence among esports players is about 60%, mostly in the spine and wrists ([systematic review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12764727/)). Among collegiate varsity players, 36% report wrist pain and injuries have caused early retirements ([collegiate study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9749791/)). Because injury accumulates with career length, it would look like an age effect.

The strongest counter-signal is that **Fortnite champions are getting older**. Winners were 16 in 2019, 16 and 19 in 2023, 16 and 17 in 2024, and in 2025 a trio that included 23-year-old Queasy ([Wikipedia: 2025 FNCS Global Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FNCS_Global_Championship)). His teammate SwizzY is listed as a repeat champion in 2026, although that listing appeared within the past day and should be treated as provisional ([Wikipedia: FNCS Global Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FNCS_Global_Championship)). This is what cohort aging plus scene maturation predicts. Kang finds that esports competitive windows "expanded considerably" as professionalization and prize pools grew ([Kang 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/simgam/v57y2026i1p93-114.html)). Peterbot adds a useful detail: he placed 37th and 26th at his first LANs, then won the 2024 LAN at 17 ([Wikipedia: Peterbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterbot)). Handling stage pressure looks like a skill that improves with experience.

The case for biology should still be made at full strength. SC2 expertise did not change the slope of age-related slowing, and Fortnite's buildfights plausibly reward raw speed more than RTS macro play does. Commentary argues fights "can often be won just by being significantly faster" ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/fortnite/why-fortnite-competitive-pros-quitting-game-alarming-rate)). If so, a 10 ms/year loop slowdown after the early 20s could matter at the elite margin even though it is invisible in average players. The comparable esports numbers support a **gradual** decline, not a cliff. In Counter-Strike, kills per round peak at 0.74 at age 20 and fall to 0.65 by 29 among HLTV Top-20 players ([HLTV](https://www.hltv.org/news/33610/when-do-counter-strike-players-peak)). League of Legends pros peak near 21 and decline "swiftly", but the authors' interviews blame burnout, role inflexibility and poor mental-health support ([TPM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1108/TPM-09-2024-0108)). Biology is therefore a plausible **minor, late-acting** contributor that a model should be able to detect. It is not the explanation for 17-year-olds fading.

| Explanation | Predicted timing | Evidence strength | How to test in data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perception-action loop slowing | Gradual, from ~20–24 | Moderate (SC2, cross-sectional) | Age slope after controlling for tenure, cohort and activity; telemetry latency trend |
| Cohort truncation / right-censoring | Artefactually early peak | Strong (chess analogue, 2017 launch) | Compare curves by debut cohort; check whether champion age is rising |
| Burnout / streaming / exit | Any age, often 16–19 | Strong anecdotal, LoL qualitative | Competing-risks exit model; drop in event volume |
| Format/meta resets | Season and chapter boundaries | Strong (7+ team-size changes) | Era random effects; veteran × new-chapter interaction |
| Physical wear | Grows with tenure | Moderate (~60% pain prevalence) | Tenure term separate from age; unannounced absences |

## Rating systems and aging curves exist, but Fortnite forecasting does not

No academic paper, thesis or public model forecasts Fortnite pro placements or careers. An arXiv search surfaced only a 2026 aimbot-detection paper that used Fortnite as a test case ([arXiv 2606.07650](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07650v1)). The closest battle royale work is the 2018 Kaggle **PUBG Finish Placement** competition. It predicted within-match placement from end-of-match stats, and gradient boosting won, helped by re-ranking predictions within each match ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/c/pubg-finish-placement-prediction/); [UT Austin project](https://cliveunger.github.io/EE460J_Final_Project_PUBG/)). That is a post-hoc task, not forward forecasting. The transferable lesson is that only the ranking within a lobby matters. Dehpanah and colleagues evaluated rating systems on **25,000+ team battle royale matches**. Evaluation metrics disagreed considerably, new players distorted most of them, and **NDCG was the most reliable** ([arXiv 2105.14069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14069)).

The rating-system toolbox for 100-player lobbies is mature. **Weng-Lin (OpenSkill)** gives closed-form Plackett-Luce updates, as accurate as TrueSkill but simpler ([JMLR 2011](https://jmlr.org/papers/v12/weng11a.html)). **Elo-MMR** is built for massive contests: it runs in linear time, is incentive-compatible, and bounds rating changes for volatile players. It processes all of Codeforces in under a minute ([arXiv 2101.00400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00400); [code](https://github.com/EbTech/Elo-MMR)). **TrueSkill 2** feeds kill counts, experience and squad membership into skill inference and raised outcome-prediction accuracy from 52% to 68% ([MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/trueskill2.pdf)). That maps directly onto Fortnite's placement-plus-eliminations scoring. Duffield, Power and Rimella show that Elo, Glicko and TrueSkill are all approximate Kalman filters in a state-space model ([JRSS-C 2024](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssc/article/73/5/1262/7734616)). This is the key conceptual bridge: an aging curve can enter a rating system as the **expected drift** of latent skill.

Epic launched its own official **Power Rankings on June 5, 2026**. It is an Elo-style rating built from a player's best 20 results over two years, with event weights, value decaying from 180 days and falling to zero at 720 days, and a rule that weaker results do not lower the rating ([Fortnite Tracker](https://fortnitetracker.com/article/2426/fortnite-adds-power-rankings-unreal-legends-rank-and-reveals-next-seasons-tournaments)). Because bad events are ignored, **PR is designed to hide decline**. It is a baseline to beat, not a target to predict. Kalshi lists an FNCS Global Championship 2026 winner market, and a few sportsbooks price outrights ([Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxfortnite/fortnite-tournament/kxfortnite-fncsgc26)). Those prices give a market-implied benchmark for forecasting Majors.

Sports aging-curve research holds the most important methodological lesson: **method choice alone moved baseball's estimated peak by about three years**. The delta method, which chains year-over-year changes, discards more than 20% of seasons and mixes survivors with average players. A symmetric quadratic pushed the peak to 29. A GAM across all player-seasons that controls for career-average performance found 27 and beat both alternatives out of sample and in 5,000 leave-career-out resamples ([Baseball Prospectus](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/59972/the-delta-method-revisited/)). Multilevel multiple imputation of seasons that dropped-out players never played moved the MLB peak from **30 (observed only) to 26 (imputed)** ([Nguyen & Matthews](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.02383v3)). A new envelope method, RACE, models each athlete's level, timing and tempo of aging relative to a population frontier and is built for sparse, irregular careers ([arXiv 2608.06635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06635)). In chess, early achievers peak earlier: players who earned the GM title before 15 peaked at 22.1, against an overall mean of 30.65 ([Erilli & Dalar 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485108/)). That makes **debut age a required covariate**. Changepoint and survival tools such as BOCPD ([Adams & MacKay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.3742)) and competing-risks hazards have not been applied to esports decline in anything found, so applying them would be novel.

## Public data covers placements well and reflexes poorly

A student can build a clean **placement panel** but only a partial **mechanics panel**.

For placements, the backbone is Epic's events and leaderboard service. It exposes per-session placement, points, eliminations and time alive, but it is an unofficial, token-authenticated endpoint that carries terms-of-service risk ([fortnitepy docs](https://fortnitepy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/fortnitepy/http.html); [endpoint docs](https://github.com/LeleDerGrasshalmi/FortniteEndpointsDocumentation)). The Cito API resells that data with kill feeds, storm circles and parsed server replays for FNCS and LAN events. It indexes 2.6M+ tournament results, has a free tier of 500 requests a month, and paid plans start at $25 ([Cito API](https://citoapi.com/fortnite/)). Liquipedia's LPDB is free only for educational, non-commercial and open-source use, under CC-BY-SA ([Liquipedia API guidelines](https://liquipedia.net/commons/Liquipedia:API_Usage_Guidelines)). A student project qualifies if the code is published. Esports Earnings offers a keyed API at 1 request per second, but it only covers prize-winning results and has **no birthdate field** ([Esports Earnings API](https://www.esportsearnings.com/apidocs)). No structured source provides ages. They must come from Wikipedia and Liquipedia infoboxes. That biases age coverage toward famous players and raises privacy concerns, because many subjects are minors.

For mechanics, Fortnite `.replay` files parsed with FortniteReplayDecompressor (v3.1.0, September 2026) contain timestamped positions with yaw, shots (weapon, damage, crits, target), damage taken, weapon switches, and the kill feed ([parser guide](https://mintlify.wiki/SL-x-TnT/FortniteReplayDecompressor/guides/extracting-player-data); [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/Shiqan/FortniteReplayDecompressor/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). The data supports several candidate Fortnite analogues of looking-doing latency. **Hit-to-return-fire latency** runs from a damage-taken event to the player's next shot. **Weapon-switch-to-first-shot latency** is another. Accuracy, headshot share and fight-level win rates can also be computed. The signature Fortnite mechanics, **building and editing, are not documented as extractable**, and most replays show only the owner's perspective. Tournament-wide replays are not public, although Cito and Osirion ([Osirion](https://osirion.gg/)) parse some of them commercially.

Two findings from other telemetry work shape the feature design. First, **intra-individual variability** (the SD, CV or ex-Gaussian tau of a player's latencies) rises with age. In clinical samples it precedes slowing of the mean and predicts outcomes years later better than mean RT does ([PubMed 20853957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20853957/); [MS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11299566/)). That makes it a candidate leading indicator, though its sensitivity between 18 and 30 is untested. Second, Sea Hero Quest data from 5,896 players **separated motor decline (speed, r = −0.47) from decision decline (goal-directedness from a fitted policy model, r = −0.14)**, and a joint model beat either proxy alone ([iScience 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12481093/)). Because the signal is small, pulling it out of within-player data will require many events per player aggregated across matches. Acute fatigue also degrades esports executive function within a session ([Matsui et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224000876)). Aging should therefore be modeled as a slow trend across seasons, and fatigue as within-session slopes.

## A two-phase project isolates age from everything that travels with it

The core design problem is identification. Age, tenure (time since debut) and birth cohort are nearly collinear in a young game, and each is also tangled with format era. This is a variant of the classic age-period-cohort identification problem, which follows from the definitions rather than from any source. The project is built so that age gets credit **only for variation that tenure, debut cohort, era and activity cannot explain**. The primary deliverable is a placement forecaster that is useful whether or not biology matters. The aging question is a secondary, falsifiable hypothesis inside it.

| Component | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Problem formulation** | Primary: forecast each player's (or team's) placement percentile and points at the next Major, LAN or Global Championship, given all results before that event's date. Secondary: estimate the hazard of "decline onset", defined as a sustained negative changepoint in smoothed latent skill, with exit treated as a competing risk. |
| **Unit of analysis** | Player × event (heats of one tournament grouped together); outcome = within-lobby percentile, not raw points, so formats stay comparable. |
| **Data pipeline** | (1) Pull FNCS, cash-cup and LAN results from Cito's free tier or Epic's leaderboards, 2019 to present. (2) Resolve identities (Epic account IDs vs changing display names) and pull debut dates and birthdates via Liquipedia LPDB, with attribution and code published open-source. (3) Hand-build an event metadata table: team size, scoring and surge rules, chapter/season, LAN vs online, region, prize pool. (4) Optional Phase 2: collect consented replays from a few pros or orgs and parse them with FortniteReplayDecompressor. Store ages as age in days and never publish minors' birthdates. |
| **Features** | Lagged latent skill and its uncertainty; recent slope; event volume in the past 90/180 days (activity/burnout proxy); tenure; debut age; debut cohort; age as a spline; team size; teammate skill; LAN indicator plus a player-specific LAN offset; days since chapter start; earnings to date (proxy for streaming income). Phase 2 adds median and IIV of hit-to-return-fire and switch-to-shot latency, accuracy and fight win rate, each residualized on weapon and distance. |
| **Model stage 1: latent skill** | Weng-Lin Plackett-Luce (OpenSkill) or Elo-MMR over full lobby orderings, plus a Poisson/negative-binomial elimination channel à la TrueSkill 2, so mechanical and strategic skill can separate. Inflate skill variance at chapter boundaries. |
| **Model stage 2: forecasting** | A hierarchical Bayesian model in PyMC or NumPyro: percentile ~ player random intercept + s(age) + s(tenure) + cohort + era random effects + activity + LAN × player. Compare against LightGBM with a LambdaMART ranking objective on the same features. |
| **Model stage 3: decline** | BOCPD or PELT on the smoothed skill series to label decline onsets. Cause-specific Cox or Fine-Gray hazards for decline vs exit, with time-varying covariates. Multilevel MICE sensitivity analysis for dropped-out players. |
| **Evaluation** | Rolling-origin CV by event date (train before event *t*, predict *t*). Metrics: NDCG@10 and @50, Spearman ρ, and Brier/log-loss on top-10 finish and qualification, with calibration plots. Add leave-player-out CV for the decline model. |
| **Baselines** | Last-event rank; rolling-mean percentile; Epic PR; Kalshi or sportsbook implied probabilities for Majors. |
| **Hypothesis test** | Does s(age) add out-of-sample skill beyond tenure, cohort, era and activity? Is the age effect centered in the 20s (biology) or tied to tenure and chapter resets (non-biological)? Set a weakly informative prior on an age-driven drift beginning around 21–24, taken from the SC2 and LoL results, and report the posterior honestly. |

Five pitfalls deserve explicit guards. **Survivorship** flattens or distorts curves when only continuing players are fit. Model exit jointly and never use the delta method. **Regression to the mean** in noisy 100-player lobbies makes one bad cup look like decline, so flag decline only from smoothed skill with credible intervals. **Teammate confounding** in duos and trios means individual decline can hide behind roster changes, which the individual elimination channel and teammate-skill terms address. **Age missingness** is not random: famous players have birthdates and mid-tier players often do not, so report which players have known ages and fit the age analysis only on that subset. **Data rights** carry real risk. Epic's internal endpoints are unsanctioned, so prefer Cito or Liquipedia for anything published.

## Conclusion

The most useful change of perspective is that Fortnite's "early peak" is mostly a property of the **game's age, not the players' age**. A title that is nine years old, has a 13-year entry floor, and resets its format every season will produce teenage champions whatever happens in anyone's basal ganglia. The rising age of recent champions is the first sign that this artefact is fading. A student who builds the forecaster above therefore gets two results from one dataset. The first is a working Major and LAN predictor that can be benchmarked against Epic's PR and prediction markets. The second is the first within-player test of whether a mid-20s perception-action slowdown shows up in battle royale competition once tenure, cohort and burnout are controlled.

If the age term adds nothing out of sample, that null is itself publishable: it would show that the popular reaction-time story is mostly survivorship and cohort effects. If the age term does add signal, the next step is Phase 2 telemetry, looking for rising latency variability (a possible early warning) in consenting pros. The biology question is answerable, but only through a design that is not built on the assumption that biology is the answer.
